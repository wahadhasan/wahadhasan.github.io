About

I am a PhD candidate in industrial engineering with a major in operations research, focused on building optimization models and algorithms that hold up under real-world uncertainty. My research sits between theory and application: I care about provable guarantees, but I test everything against real operational data before I trust it.

Currently, I am working on developing an algorithmic framework for two-stage distributionally robust bilevel optimization problem. Before this, I studied large-scale decomposition algorithms and proposed a novel decomposition framework for two-stage and multi-stage stochastic programs. I also have strong expertise in heuristic algorithms and applied them in inventory optimization and network design problems. Currently, I split my time between methodological research and applied collaborations with industry partners in real-world logistics and supply chain problems.

Course and research projects

Selected research projects

  • [R1]

    A Branch-and-Price-and-Cut Framework for the Multi-Compartment Vehicle Routing Problem

    Developed an exact branch-and-price-and-cut algorithm for optimizing multi-compartment vehicle routes under complex capacity constraints. Implemented the optimization model using Gurobi and evaluated its performance on challenging routing instances, achieving high-quality solutions with improved computational efficiency

  • [R2]

    An optimized hub-and-spoke recycling configuration for selected rural communities in Arizona

    Developed a mathematical optimization model to design a hub-and-spoke recycling network across six rural Arizona counties. Evaluated alternative hub locations and material flows to identify cost-effective network configurations, with the goal of improving recycling accessibility and transportation efficiency in underserved communities.

    The optimized network was evaluated under alternative recycling diversion rates while holding other model parameters constant. The sensitivity analysis is performed to examine the revenue structure across candidate hub locations across the communities and evaluates the robustness of the network economics to changes in material diversion.

  • [R3]

    Demographic and Spatial Analysis of Recycling Infrastructure in Rural Arizona

    Collected and analyzed demographic data for different communities in Arizona and mapped existing recycling facility locations, including transfer stations, recovery facilities, and landfills, using SQL and Excel, to assess geographic coverage and accessibility. Developed an interactive HTML-based visualization to explore community demographics, facility locations, and spatial patterns across the study area.

Course projects

  • [C1]

    Combinatorial Benders cuts for assembly line balancing problems

    Implemented a combinatorial Benders decomposition approach based on an existing research formulation to solve assembly line balancing problems. Performed numerical experiments for different test sizes and evaluated their performance on benchmark instances.

  • [C2]

    Non-parametric change point detection methods for multivariate time series data

    Investigated non-parametric methods for detecting structural changes in multivariate time series data. Implemented and compared change point detection techniques to identify shifts in underlying data patterns without assuming a specific probability distribution.
